The Week Ahead in the Sandhills

Updated Sunday, November 1

What is on across Pinehurst, Southern Pines, Aberdeen and the rest of Moore County over the coming week — two gallery shows opening on the same Friday night, the last outdoor concert of the year, a Veterans Day parade down Broad Street, and a couple of days quiet enough to spend in the woods.

It stays unusually warm for early November, with the only wet spell arriving midweek. The live forecast below the listings is the one to trust.

The week, day by day

Sunday, November 1

  • Last day of the Sandhills Scarecrow Stroll, Sandhills Horticultural Gardens, Pinehurst, 8am–7pm. Free, and you can still vote for a favourite.
  • Cars and Coffee with the Sandhills Motoring Club, Village of Pinehurst, 9–11am. Free; park in front of the Roast Office and through the Village.
  • Last day of Kalawi Farm’s Hello Autumn festival, 1515 NC Highway 211, Eagle Springs, 10am–7pm — corn maze, super slide and hayrides to the pumpkin patch. $8 adults, $15 ages 3–17, free under 2. About twenty minutes out, and the peach ice cream is made with fruit grown there.
  • Pop Up in the Pines Fall Market, Druther’s, 490 SW Broad Street, Southern Pines, 11am–4pm. Free — makers, food trucks and live music.
  • Whiskey Pines play Red’s Corner, SW Broad Street, Southern Pines, 1–3pm. Beer garden and food trucks; their app carries the day’s line-up.
  • Monthly meditation walk, Sandhills Horticultural Gardens, 2–3pm. Free, silent, meeting on the visitor centre patio. Cancelled if it rains, and no dogs in the garden.

Monday, November 2

  • Nothing on the public calendar. A good afternoon for the two-mile loop around the lake at Reservoir Park, which also has fishing, kayaking and an 18-hole disc golf course if you want to make a morning of it. Or walk Pinehurst Village, which is at its best early or late in the day, when it is less crowded.
  • Worth knowing: the Campbell House galleries are between exhibitions until Friday.

Tuesday, November 3

  • Quiet again, and the clearest day of the week. Weymouth Woods Sandhills Nature Preserve opens at 8am — 900 acres, six easy trails, and a boardwalk over swampland. The Lighter Stump–Pine Island–Holly Road loop is 3.8 miles through longleaf pines, some of them close to 500 years old. Free. Dogs on a six-foot leash.

Wednesday, November 4

  • The Cradle Knockout, Pinehurst — registration from 4:30pm, $10 cash, limited to the first hundred. Three rounds on the short course: hit the green, land inside twenty feet, closest to the pin.
  • Max Brallier presents and signs “The Last Comics on Earth: Across the Doodleverse”, The Country Bookshop, 140 NW Broad Street, Southern Pines. Games and activities 5:30pm, the talk at 6pm, questions at 6:20pm, signing from 6:40pm. Free, but registration is required.
  • Uncorked, Cork, 143 NE Broad Street, Southern Pines, 7–9pm. Four two-ounce pours from around the world and a full glass of the one you liked, for sixteen people a week. There is a charge per person and it books out quickly.

Thursday, November 5

  • Shaw House grounds and buildings, 110 Morganton Road, Southern Pines, 1–4pm. Free tours, all ages, Thursday to Saturday.
  • Stephen Proctor on Joyce Wethered, Glenna Collett and the rise of women’s golf — a fireside chat at The USGA Experience and World Golf Hall of Fame, 3 Carolina Vista Drive, Pinehurst, 6–7pm. Free; doors at 5:30pm, light refreshments, registration required.
  • American Mahjong with Pine & Tiles, Poplar Place, downtown Aberdeen, 6:30pm. Guided play $30 with a coach at the table, open play $10. Everything you need is provided; come alone or bring people.

All week, whenever you like

Both of Friday’s gallery openings then settle in for the rest of the year. “Native Identity” hangs free at the Arts Council’s Campbell House galleries, 482 E Connecticut Ave, Southern Pines, weekdays 10am–5pm to December 19 — closed on Veterans Day. The Artists League of the Sandhills shows about 150 new works at the Exchange Street Gallery, 129 Exchange Street, Aberdeen, Monday to Saturday 12–3pm, to December 18. Both are a short walk from a coffee shop, and both are good wet-afternoon answers along with the Broad Street walk — The Country Bookshop, the antique shops and the restored 1920s Sunrise Theater — and Rec Room’s arcade games.

Downtown Aberdeen runs a social district: pick up a drink at a “Beverages Sold Here” bar and carry it into the “Beverages Welcome” shops and streets nearby, Thursday to Saturday 11am–11pm and Sunday 1–9pm. Free and open to all; 21 and over to buy or carry a drink.

Outdoors, Weymouth Woods opens at 8am daily, free, with the 3.8-mile Lighter Stump–Pine Island–Holly Road loop the route to take. Rassie Wicker Park in Pinehurst has paved greenway trails, easy walking before coffee. With a dog: Martin Park, 350 Commerce Ave, Southern Pines, is 50 off-leash acres of wooded trails and creek, dawn to dusk and free — only partly fenced, so your dog needs good recall, and park in the cul-de-sac rather than the Yadkin Plaza lot, where you risk being towed. Pooch Park in the Pines in Whispering Pines is 3.5 fully fenced acres with separate small and large sides; it is members-only, but worth asking about a day pass. More on visiting with a pet.

Eat & drink

  • Char Bar No. 7, Southern Pines — a covered patio with televisions and winter heat. The answer when the weather is bad but you still want to sit outside, and a dog is welcome at the outdoor tables.
  • The Sly Fox, 795 SW Broad St, Southern Pines — British-style gastropub, 16 taps. Shepherd’s pie, poutine, bangers and mash. Low-key, warm and unhurried when the weather turns.
  • Betsy’s Crepes, Southern Pines — quick service, sweet and savoury. Nutella and banana.
  • Mulligans Creamery — small-batch ice cream that leans all the way into the golf theme: scoops come as a bogey, a double bogey or a birdie. Vegan and dairy-free options.
  • Agora Bakery + Cafe, Pinehurst — family-run, inside a historic bank building, more bakery than coffee shop. The spinach pie is excellent, and the macarons have a following. Go here if you want coffee with a real meal.
  • Railhouse Brewery, downtown Aberdeen — brewing since 2010, veteran-owned, 16 taps plus full liquor. Cheese curds and the soft pretzel are the standouts, it is family and dog friendly, and it sits inside the social district. Five minutes from Pinehurst.
  • Smoke and Barrel, Southern Pines — slow-cooked meats, beer cheese with pretzels, a deep bourbon list.
  • Rec Room Bar and Arcade, Southern Pines — Pac-Man and Galaga alongside the craft beer.
  • The Deuce, Pinehurst No. 2 clubhouse, 1 Carolina Vista Dr — a terrace over the 18th green. You do not need a tee time on No. 2 to drink here; just turn up. Get the wings.

Out on the courses

Nothing closed this week, and no aeration or overseeding. The Cradle — eighteen par-threes on the resort grounds, and the easiest way onto the property without a full tee time — gives itself over to the monthly Knockout from 4:30pm on Wednesday November 4, $10 cash and the first hundred to register, so a late loop that day should go earlier. You do not need a resort package to play well around here.

Getting around

Columbus Drive in Aberdeen stays closed near Aberdeen Pines Way, between U.S. 15-501 and South Hill Road, until November 30, detoured via U.S. 15-501, Johnson Street and South Hill Road. Single lanes on U.S. 15-501 between Turner Street and U.S. 1 close nightly, 8pm to 6am, to the same date. Midland Road is still being milled and resurfaced between Airport Road and Clark Street, with periodic lane closures in off-peak daytime hours, so allow a few extra minutes between Pinehurst and Southern Pines in the middle of the day.

On Saturday November 7, downtown Southern Pines belongs to the Veterans Parade from mid-morning: the opening ceremony is at 9:30am and the parade steps off at 10am, in the same blocks as the Saturday farmers market. Be parked before 9:30. Thursday November 12 brings the Tour de Trike to Broad Street at 5:30pm, which fills the sidewalks for an hour.
