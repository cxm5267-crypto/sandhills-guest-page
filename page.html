The Week Ahead in the Sandhills

Updated Sunday, October 11

What's on across Pinehurst, Southern Pines, Aberdeen and the rest of Moore County over the next seven days — markets, music, galleries, festivals and a good deal of October. Times and prices are as the organisers published them; the forecast above is pulled live from the National Weather Service.

The short version: a warm, dry run of days, climbing to the high 80s midweek before the first properly cool night of the month arrives at the weekend. Saturday is the big one, with the 48th Holly Arts & Crafts Festival filling the Village of Pinehurst from 10am.

All week, whenever you like

Three gallery shows are up and all of them are free. “Driven Beauty” — photographs of toy cars that read as real places, paintings so exact they pass for photographs, and layered mixed-media car imagery — hangs at the Arts Council Galleries in Campbell House, 482 E. Connecticut Ave, Southern Pines, weekdays 10am–5pm to October 30, plus Saturday October 17 from 10am to 2pm. The Artists League of the Sandhills shows “Inspired by Land & Sea” at the Exchange Street Gallery of Fine Art in Aberdeen, Monday to Saturday 12–3pm, to October 23. And “Frank Pierce: Crossroads” has its last day on Monday at the Jeanne Hastings Gallery inside the Katharine Boyd Library at Sandhills Community College.

The Sandhills Scarecrow Stroll runs daily 8am to 7pm at the Sandhills Horticultural Gardens in Pinehurst, free, to November 1 — this year's scarecrows are Revolutionary-era characters for the country's 250th. Carolina Cinemas keeps Flashback Cinema going at 7:30pm every night: “Beetlejuice” through Thursday, “Clue” from Friday to the following Thursday, then “Scream”. After dark there is a drive-by haunted cemetery on Oakmont Circle in Pinehurst, free, 7–9pm — enjoy it from the street rather than the yard.

The mornings this week are made for walking. Weymouth Woods Sandhills Nature Preserve opens at 8am, free, with four-plus miles of easy trail through longleaf pines, some of them estimated at nearly 500 years old; the Lighter Stump to Pine Island to Holly Road loop is 3.8 miles and takes in a boardwalk over swampland. In Pinehurst, Rassie Wicker Park has paved county greenway trails that make a flat, easy walk before coffee. Dogs are welcome on a six-foot leash at Weymouth Woods; if you have brought one, Martin Park in Southern Pines is 50 acres of off-leash woods and fields — only partly fenced, so your dog needs good recall, and park in the cul-de-sac rather than the Yadkin Plaza lot, where you risk being towed.

Eat & drink

  • Drum & Quill, Pinehurst Village — pub food and ten outdoor tables. Good fish and chips, and the fried okra with Green Goddess dressing is worth the trip on its own. Open late at the weekend, live music Thursday.
  • BRIM, Pinehurst — in the historic Outpost building next to the Given Tufts Bookshop, in the Village where Saturday's festival sets up. Espresso imported from Italy, Cactus Creek for drip, New York-style bagels from Delucia's in Seven Lakes, and gelato worth an afternoon stop.
  • Smoke and Barrel, Southern Pines — slow-cooked meats, beer cheese with pretzels and a deep bourbon list. The right answer on a 43° Saturday night.
  • Railhouse Brewery, downtown Aberdeen — brewing since 2010, veteran-owned, 16 taps plus full liquor. Cheese curds and the soft pretzel are the standouts. Family and dog friendly, five minutes from Pinehurst, and the home of Sunday's car show.
  • The Ice Cream Parlor, Broad Street, Southern Pines — a real soda fountain, on that street since 1976. Honey lavender in a cone. Expect a line, especially early evening.
  • The Deuce, at the Pinehurst No. 2 clubhouse — a terrace over the 18th green. You do not need a tee time on No. 2 to drink here; just turn up. Get the wings.
  • Agora Bakery + Cafe, Pinehurst — family-run, inside a historic bank building, more bakery than coffee shop. The spinach pie is excellent. Go here if you want coffee with a real meal.

Out on the courses

Seven Lakes Golf Club has an 18-hole charity scramble on Monday with a 10am shotgun start, so that course is tied up for the morning. Nothing else has changed: no closures, no aeration and no tournament on the resort courses. If you are looking for a round, you do not need a resort package to play well here — there are eighteen courses you can play without staying at the resort, and three top-100 public courses within reach. The Cradle, 18 par-threes on the resort grounds, is short, walkable and the easiest way onto the property without a full tee time.

Getting around

Columbus Drive in Aberdeen is closed near Aberdeen Pines Way, between U.S. 15-501 and South Hill Road, for storm drainage work; it is scheduled to reopen on November 30, and the signed detour runs U.S. 15-501, Johnson Street and South Hill Road. On the same project, single lanes of U.S. 15-501 between Turner Street and U.S. 1 close nightly from 8pm to 6am, also through November 30.

Midland Road between Airport Road and Clark Street is being milled and resurfaced, with periodic lane closures during off-peak daytime hours. No detour is posted; allow a few extra minutes driving between Pinehurst and Southern Pines.

Saturday is the day to think about parking. The Holly Arts & Crafts Festival takes the Village of Pinehurst from 10am to 4pm, and the farmers market holds the Downtown Park in Southern Pines until noon. Both are easiest well before 10am. The Village is at its best early anyway, when it is less crowded.
